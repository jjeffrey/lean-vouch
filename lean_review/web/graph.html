How to read this diagram

Each arrow points from a declaration to one it depends on, with the arrows implied by others left out (a Hasse diagram). For a theorem, only its statement counts; for a definition, its statement and body. Colors are your marks; a solid orange border means it changed since a mark that relies on it, a dashed one that something below it did; selecting one traces the way down to the change. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan. Click a node to see its code and notes, and to highlight what it depends on and what depends on it; click empty space to clear. Boxes are folders and modules, with how many of their declarations you have marked: click one to open it, and click an open box's label to close it. A thicker arrow between boxes stands for more dependencies. Arrows are drawn between neighbors in the same folder; "arrows across folders" draws the rest. Shift-click anything, a declaration or a box, to color everything it depends on and everything that uses it, however far away. Cmd- or Ctrl-click in the list to show only the folders and modules you pick, however distant, and the arrows between them. A red border means a declaration contains sorry, and a dashed one that it relies on one, even if only through a proof; dashed red arrows lead to the sorry step by step. Shift-click a sorry to see all it reaches.
